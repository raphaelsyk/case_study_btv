### What export control, licensing, or trade policy constraints are disclosed, and how do they affect guidance (e.g. excluded revenue, revenue-share terms)?

New export controls on the H20 chip issued on April 9 resulted in a loss of $2.5 billion in unshippered Q1 revenue, a $4.5 billion inventory write-down charge, and an expected $8 billion revenue loss in Q2.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 3 | Colette M. Kress | We were unable to ship $2.5 billion in H20 revenue in the first quarter due to the new export controls. The $4.5 billion charge was less than what we initially anticipated, as we were able to reuse certain materials. |

### How does management characterize the durability/predictability of these constraints (one-off vs. ongoing uncertainty)?

Management views these export constraints as a severe and ongoing threat that forces them to evaluate limited options while permanently closing access to a major market unless policy evolves.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 7 | Jensen Huang | Today, however, the $50 billion China market is effectively closed to US industry. The H20 export ban ended our Hopper Data Center business in China. We cannot reduce Hopper further to comply. |
