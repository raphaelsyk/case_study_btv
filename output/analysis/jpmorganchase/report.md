## Framing

- The discussion of AI evolved from being proactively raised by management in 2025_Q1 as a standard part of their investment agenda to being exclusively reactive to analyst questions in 2025_Q2, 2025_Q3, 2026_Q1, and 2026_Q2. In 2025_Q4, it was mentioned in response to a question but proactively listed as an opportunity area. [1][2][3][4][5]
- AI's framing shifted significantly over the period. Initially presented as a general, long-term technology investment in 2025_Q1, it was viewed as a usable tool in 2025_Q2. By 2025_Q3, the focus narrowed to AI as an efficiency tool for controlling headcount growth. In 2025_Q4, it was framed as a competitive necessity whose efficiency gains would be passed to customers. This view was reinforced in 2026_Q1 and 2026_Q2, with an added emphasis on using AI to enhance client services and create new product adjacencies. [1][6][7][8][9][10]
- Management's perspective on AI's net effect changed from neutral/unspecified in 2025_Q1 and 2025_Q2 to an opportunity for productivity gains in 2025_Q3 and a 'huge opportunity' in 2025_Q4. A more balanced view emerged in 2026_Q1, acknowledging both significant opportunities and substantial risks like cybersecurity. By 2026_Q2, the focus returned to AI as a significant opportunity, with risks not being mentioned in the discussion. [11][12][13][14][15]
- The tone of the AI discussion evolved from neutral and resolute in 2025_Q1 to pragmatic and dismissive of acquiring an LLM in 2025_Q2. A cautious and pragmatic tone was adopted in 2025_Q3, warning against hype. This pragmatism continued through 2025_Q4 and into 2026_Q1, which also introduced cautious optimism. By 2026_Q2, the tone became more engaged, describing AI as a 'mini-revolution' while remaining pragmatic about who benefits financially. [1][6][15][16][17]

## Execution & Investment

- Across all six quarters, management consistently avoided disclosing specific investment figures for AI, using phrases like 'spending a lot of money' in 2025_Q3 and 'will be spending more' in 2025_Q4. In 2026_Q2, a 'meaningful acceleration' in 'token expense' was mentioned for the first time, though the full-year impact was described as 'trivial'. [1][18][19][20]
- Characterization of AI's ROI evolved. After no mention of ROI in 2025_Q1 and 2025_Q2, management in 2025_Q3 described ROI as hard to prove, with benefits showing up in constrained expense growth. From 2025_Q4 through 2026_Q2, a consistent theme emerged: AI's efficiency gains would be a competitive necessity, with benefits ultimately passed to customers rather than retained as higher margins. [9][21][22][23]
- Specific AI use cases were not mentioned until 2026_Q1, which named risk management, fraud reduction, prospecting, and testing the 'Mythos' model for cybersecurity. The 'AI cash tool' was also introduced as an 'experiment'. The level of detail increased significantly in 2026_Q2, with mentions of almost 1,000 use cases, 50 of which were important, across areas like marketing, hedging, and note-taking. [24][25][26]
- Discussion of the company's AI sourcing strategy first appeared in 2025_Q2, stating a reliance on external, 'agnostic' use of LLMs. In 2025_Q4, the narrative shifted to 'building more AI systems' internally. The strategy appeared to evolve to a hybrid approach by 2026_Q1, referencing testing of an external model ('Mythos'), and was confirmed in 2026_Q2 with talk of using the 'right models for the right purpose', including open source. [6][27][28][29]
- The workforce impact of AI was first mentioned in 2025_Q3, where AI-driven productivity was cited as a reason to 'constrain headcount growth'. The first mention of actual job reductions occurred in 2026_Q2, where management disclosed that 'discrete areas' had seen job reductions of 30-40%, with most affected employees being retrained and redeployed. [30][31]

## Competitive Landscape

- Management's positioning relative to peers on AI changed over time. After no comment in the first half of 2025, they positioned the firm as experienced and ahead of the trend in 2025_Q3. In 2025_Q4, the goal was to be 'best in the world in tech'. By 2026_Q1 and 2026_Q2, the message was more nuanced, suggesting that any lead would be temporary as competitors would also adopt AI, making it a matter of keeping pace. [32][33][34][35]
- Non-traditional competitors were only mentioned in relation to AI in 2025_Q2, when an analyst brought up Apple's potential moves in the space. Management did not engage on this point. In 2025_Q4, fintechs were named as general competitors in the context of justifying tech spend, which included AI. In 2026_Q2, fintechs were mentioned again as having access to AI through vendors. [36][37][38]

## Outlook & Credibility

- Across the entire period, management consistently avoided making specific, quantifiable, or time-bound pledges about its AI efforts. Commitments remained vague, from pledging to continue investment in 2025_Q1 to building more systems in 2025_Q4, with no specific targets, timelines, or deliverables ever provided. [1][27][39]
- Management's outlook on the broader AI landscape was absent until 2025_Q3, when they acknowledged the 'overwhelming' AI theme in the marketplace. A consistent outlook emerged from 2025_Q4 to 2026_Q2, predicting that AI would become a standard competitive tool whose efficiency benefits would be passed on to customers rather than boosting bank margins. [17][38][40][41]
- The specificity of AI-related claims evolved. Initially very vague in 2025_Q1, 2025_Q2, and 2025_Q3, claims gained some qualitative detail in 2025_Q4 and 2026_Q1. By 2026_Q2, claims became more concrete, with management quantifying the number of use cases and the percentage of job reductions in certain areas, though financial details remained vague. [1][42][43][44]
