### Is AI framed primarily as a revenue/product driver, an efficiency/cost tool, or a competitive necessity?

Management primarily frames AI as an efficiency and cost-control tool. The discussion focuses on how AI can generate productivity tailwinds to help constrain headcount growth and offset other expense drivers, rather than its role in generating revenue or new products.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 8 | Jeremy Barnum | What we're saying instead is let's just do old-fashioned expense discipline and constrain people's growth - constrain people's headcount growth. We've talked about that last year. We're going to do the same this year, have a very strong bias against having the reflective (sic) [reflexive] response to any given need to be to hire more people and feeling a little bit more confident on our ability to put that pressure on the organization, because we know that even if we can't always measure it that precisely, there are definitely productivity tailwinds from AI. So, that's how we're going to do it, and hopefully, that'll show up in lower growth than we would have had otherwise. |

### Does management frame AI's net effect on the business as more opportunity or more risk?

Management frames AI's net effect as an opportunity, specifically as a source of productivity gains. They also note the risk of misapplying AI inefficiently or being distracted from more fundamental process improvements if the focus is solely on chasing AI savings without proper discipline.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 8 | Jeremy Barnum | we know that even if we can't always measure it that precisely, there are definitely productivity tailwinds from AI. |
| 2 | yes | 7 | Jeremy Barnum | what we're doing is kind of rather than saying, you must prove that you're generating this much savings from AI, which turns out to be a very hard thing to do, hard to prove and might, at the margin, result in |
| 3 | yes | 8 | Jeremy Barnum | people scrambling around to use AI in ways that are actually not efficient and that distract you from doing underlying process reengineering that you need to do. |

### What tone does management convey when discussing AI (e.g. defensive, cautious, hopeful, excited)?

Management's tone when discussing AI is pragmatic and cautious. The CFO emphasizes the need to stay 'anchored in facts and reality and tangible outcomes' rather than getting caught up in the market 'hype'. They are hopeful about productivity gains but are careful to manage expectations about directly measurable savings.
