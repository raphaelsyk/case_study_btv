# JPMorganChase: 2026_Q2 — Extracted AI-Discussion Evidence

## framing

AI is discussed on the call only in response to direct questions from analysts. CEO Jamie Dimon and CFO Jeremy Barnum both respond to analyst inquiries regarding AI's impact on expenses, efficiency, and investment.

### Is AI framed primarily as a revenue/product driver, an efficiency/cost tool, or a competitive necessity?

Management frames AI as both a significant efficiency tool and a competitive necessity whose ultimate benefits will accrue to customers rather than the firm itself. CEO Jamie Dimon highlights AI's potential for "huge efficiency" and notes that while the firm can gain a temporary advantage, in a competitive market, these benefits will lead to lower costs and error rates for clients. CFO Jeremy Barnum also mentions that through AI, the firm expects to gain more capacity, efficiency, or better revenue outcomes to compete more effectively.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 12 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | We are going to use AI to do a better job for clients. That's our job. We fully expect it'll have huge efficiency in certain parts of the company. |
| 2 | yes | 7 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | And AI still remains to be seen, because the other thing I think about AI, which is a little bit different than everybody else, is you don't uniquely benefit from AI. The ultimate beneficiary of AI will be our customers. And in a competitive capitalist world, we all will use AI to do a better job for the customers, and we can't just say, oh, it's going to increase our margins and we're going to keep that. |
| 3 | yes | 19 | Jeremy Barnum (Chief Financial Officer, JPMorganChase) | Either we're going to have a lot more capacity or we're going to have a lot more efficiency or both, or we're going to have better revenue outcomes or we're going to compete more effectively, and we just need to be disciplined about how we handle that. |

### Does management frame AI's net effect on the business as more opportunity or more risk?

Management frames AI as a significant opportunity for the business, describing it as a "mini-revolution" that will be "faster" and more "dramatic" than previous technological shifts. The discussion focuses on efficiency gains, better client service, and numerous use cases, without mentioning specific risks associated with AI.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 12 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | This will be faster. This will be dramatic. The whole company is involved in it. We have our offsite in July. But we're kind of in the midst of this mini-revolution, and we'll report to you. |

Management conveys a pragmatic and engaged tone when discussing AI. At the same time, he is cautious about the direct financial benefits, repeatedly stating that in a competitive market, the ultimate beneficiary will be the customer, not the company's profit margins.

## execution_investment

### What investment (capex/opex) is disclosed for AI?

Management references spending "quite a bit of money" on AI but does not disclose a specific total investment figure. However, CFO Jeremy Barnum mentions that the company is forecasting a "meaningful acceleration" in 'token expense' for the second half of the year, although the full-year contribution remains 'trivial'. Additionally, CEO Jamie Dimon notes that overall US AI capex is projected to grow from $700 billion this year to over $1 trillion next year, implying a significant area of economy-wide investment.

| # | Grounded | Page | Speaker | Excerpt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | yes | 12 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | I think we've mentioned in the past, we spend quite a bit of money on it. We have a lot of NPVs that we know we have. |
| 2 | yes | 19 | Jeremy Barnum (Chief Financial Officer, JPMorganChase) | We are forecasting some meaningful acceleration in that number for the second half of the year. But still nonetheless, the full year contribution of that is still trivial, and obviously, we had budgeted some of that. So, it's not in any way a meaningful driver of the current outlook or to the revision in the outlook. |
| 3 | yes | 14 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | To give you the big numbers, I think CapEx is about $4 trillion a year, and AI went from $400 billion last year to $700 billion this year. People project, so do our people, it'll be like a little over $1 trillion next year, and maybe a little reduction in the non-AI CapEx. |

CEO Jamie Dimon repeatedly cautions that while AI will create efficiencies, the benefits will ultimately be passed on to customers due to competitive pressures, rather than being retained as higher margins for the firm.

### What internal AI use cases are named, and what is their maturity (prototype / production / scaled)?

The maturity is described as "just starting," implying many are in early stages. One specific example of a scaled use case is mentioned where jobs were reduced by 30-40% in a discrete area. CFO Jeremy Barnum also mentions summarizing analyst reports as a current, practical application.

## competitive_landscape

### Does management position the company as ahead of, behind, or on par with peers in AI?

Management's positioning is nuanced. CEO Jamie Dimon suggests that while a company might get "ahead of other people kind of," any advantage is likely temporary because competitors are pursuing the same technology. He also notes that smaller competitors will have access to AI through vendors like Fiserv and FIS. This implies the firm aims to be a leader but operates in a rapidly evolving and competitive landscape where sustained leadership is challenging.

| 2 | yes | 12 | Jamie Dimon (Chairman & Chief Executive Officer, JPMorganChase) | I also think that over time, remember, this will be offered to smaller competitors too through Fiserv and FIS and other fintech-kind companies. |
