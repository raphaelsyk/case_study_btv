Quarters covered: 2025_Q4, 2026_Q1, 2026_Q2

## Framing

- The framing of AI has evolved from a dual focus on product (Erica) and efficiency in 2025_Q4 to primarily an efficiency and cost-saving tool in 2026_Q1, before expanding again in 2026_Q2 to also include indirect revenue generation through financing the broader AI infrastructure build. [1][2][3][4]
- Throughout the period, management consistently framed AI as a net opportunity for the business, focusing on productivity and efficiency benefits. [5][6][7]
- Management proactively brought up AI in prepared remarks in all three quarters. [8][9][10]
- While management's tone was consistently optimistic about AI, a note of caution regarding data security and careful management became more explicit by 2026_Q1 and 2026_Q2. [11][12][13]

## Execution & Investment

- The company's disclosure of AI investment shifted from a specific figure of 'several hundred million dollars' in 2025_Q4 to no specific figure in 2026_Q1 and 2026_Q2, where it was mentioned only as part of general technology spend. [14]
- While consistently attributing cost savings and productivity gains to AI, the quantification of these returns became less specific over time. A claim of saving 'about 2,000 people' in coding in 2025_Q4 was followed by more qualitative statements about 'real benefits' in 2026_Q1 and 'good economics' in 2026_Q2. [15][16][17]
- Reporting on internal AI use cases matured significantly. The discussion evolved from '15, 20 projects' in 2025_Q4, to '90 installations' in 2026_Q1, and then to a detailed accounting of '300 AI use cases approved' with '114 live' in 2026_Q2. [18][19][20]
- The company consistently reported a hybrid approach to AI development, leveraging external partner products like Microsoft's 365 CoPilot in 2025_Q4 while also developing internal capabilities and emphasizing data protection, a point reiterated in 2026_Q1. [21][22][23]
- While direct headcount reduction was attributed to AI in 2025_Q4, this link became less direct in later quarters. In 2026_Q1 AI was said to enable headcount management through attrition, and by 2026_Q2, no direct link between headcount changes and AI was made. [24][25][26]
- No new roles, teams, or organizational structures for AI were mentioned in any of the three quarters, though 'catalyst efforts' were noted in 2026_Q1. [27]

## Competitive Landscape

- The company's positioning on AI relative to peers evolved from no direct comparison in 2025_Q4 to implying a superior approach to data protection in 2026_Q1, and finally to more direct claims of being 'ahead of peers' and 'leading' in certain areas by 2026_Q2. [28][29][30]
- Across all three quarters, management did not name any non-traditional competitors as AI-enabled threats. [31]

## Outlook & Credibility

- The company's forward-looking statements on AI became less specific over time. Pledges in 2025_Q4 about getting more out of coding tools and the audit team's plans were followed by more general trends in 2026_Q1 and a lack of a detailed roadmap in 2026_Q2, noting simply that 'new capabilities [are] coming on every week'. [32][33][34][35]
- Management's commentary on the broader AI market appeared for the first time in 2026_Q2, where they identified the AI-driven infrastructure build as a durable economic force and a business opportunity, after not commenting on it in the prior two quarters. [36][37]
- The specificity of AI-related claims peaked in 2026_Q2. After a mix of specific and vague claims in 2025_Q4 and mostly vague claims in 2026_Q1, the company provided hard metrics in 2026_Q2 such as 300 approved use cases, 114 live GenAI cases, and 400,000 daily prompts. [38][39][40][41]
